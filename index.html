3M − 1M spread: rolling 10-day z-score

Each day's spread (VIX 3M − VIX 1M) minus its mean over the last 10 trading days, divided by the standard deviation over the same 10 days. It measures how unusual today's term-structure move is against the recent run, not the level: a positive score means the curve steepened toward contango relative to its 10-day average, a negative score that it flattened or flipped toward backwardation. Beyond ±2 is rare (about 5% of days if moves were normal) and marks a regime shift in progress.

10-day z-score of 3M − 1M above 0: steepening (toward contango) below 0: flattening (toward backwardation) ±2 bands

Z-score distribution

Every daily 10-day z-score of the 3M − 1M spread since the series began, as a histogram, against the standard normal curve N(0, 1) and a normal fitted to the data. Two things make the fit poor by construction. A z-score whose own day is inside its 10-day window can never exceed ±2.85 (9 ÷ √10), so the normal's far tails are unreachable. And the spread is a persistent series, so today's value tends to sit at the edge of its own window, pushing scores outward from the centre. Term structure

Model-free volatility of each expiry. Each index is interpolated between the two expiries either side of its horizon: 30 days for 1M, 93 days for 3M.

By expiry

Option strip

The out-of-the-money options that make up each term's variance, and each strike's share of it.

How it is calculated
  • Per expiry. σ² = (2/T) Σ (ΔK/K²) eRT Q(K) − (1/T)(F/K₀ − 1)², using the bid/ask mid of out-of-the-money puts below K₀ and calls above it, and the put/call average at K₀. The forward F comes from put-call parity at the strike where call and put prices are closest. The strip stops after two consecutive strikes with no usable bid.
  • 30-day (1M) index. Total variance is interpolated linearly in time between the expiries either side of 30 days, then annualized: 100 × √(σ²₃₀). Expiries closer than 2 days are dropped. (TFEX lists monthly expiries only, so the classic 7-day roll left both remaining expiries beyond 30 days on a quarter of all days; extrapolating from them amplifies the uneven spacing of neighbouring expiries' volatilities and caused jumps on roll days. On two years of data the 2-day roll cut extrapolated days from 120 to 41 and the worst roll-day jump from 4.3 to 2.5 points.)
  • 3-month index. The same interpolation to 93 days (the Cboe VIX3M convention), between the expiries either side of 93 days. TFEX lists four expiries (three consecutive months and the next quarter-end), so 93 days is bracketed on all but a handful of days each year, when the farthest expiry is 92 days out and the index extrapolates from the last two. A 3M above 1M (contango) is the usual state; 1M above 3M (backwardation) marks stress, as on 4 Mar 2026 when 1M reached 33.7 against a 3M of 18.7.
  • Spread z-score. For each settlement day, the 3M − 1M spread minus its mean over the trailing 10 trading days (that day included), divided by the sample standard deviation of the same 10 spreads. Settlement values only, so both legs come from the same price source. It scores the change in the curve against its recent behaviour, not the curve's level: the spread itself says whether the curve is in contango or backwardation.
  • Z-score distribution. The histogram bins every daily 10-day z-score in steps of 0.25 and overlays the expected count under the standard normal (and under a normal with the data's own mean and SD). A z-score whose own day is part of its 10-day window is bounded at ±(n − 1)/√n = ±2.85, and even for independent normal spreads its exact distribution is narrower than N(0, 1) (SD 0.95, about 1.2% of days below −2 and 1.2% above +2, rather than 2.3% on each side). The spread is far from independent: its day-to-day autocorrelation is close to 0.9, which by itself widens the z-score distribution and thins its tails. The comparison column therefore simulates a spread with that autocorrelation and no information, computed exactly as the page computes the real one.
  • History. Every trading day since 11 Sep 2024 is recomputed with the same formula from TFEX's daily settlement prices, which exist for every listed series, traded or not. TFEX floors far out-of-the-money settlements at the 0.10 tick; those count as a zero bid, so the strip stops after two of them in a row. The expiry-day row, which TFEX reports as the final index value, is excluded. TFEX's site keeps about two years online; the collector keeps its own copy, so the series keeps growing. Settlement-based and quote-based values are not identical: TFEX's settlement prices and the bid/ask mids of thinly quoted options can differ, so the live point may sit a point or so off the settlement line.
  • Strict vs tail-completed. TradingView lists back months over a narrow strike range (e.g. 1010–1140), while options at the edge are still worth several points, so the strict strip cuts off part of the tail and understates variance. The tail-completed version extends each wing in 10-point steps with Black-76 prices at the last quoted strike's IV, held flat, until the premium falls below 0.05. Flat IV is conservative: the put skew usually keeps rising, so the true value is at or slightly above the headline.
  • Inputs. Option quotes from TradingView's options screener (TFEX, 15-minute delayed). SET50 Index Futures: TradingView's continuous front-month series TFEX:S501!, unadjusted, daily close. Risk-free rate: Thailand 1-year government bond yield. Expiry time: 16:30 Bangkok on the last trading day. Realized volatility: 21-day standard deviation of daily log returns, × √252.
  • Caveats. TFEX options are thinly quoted; some far strikes carry placeholder markets (bid 0.1 / ask 373) and are excluded when the spread exceeds both 5 points and the option's own mid. This is an independent calculation, not an official TFEX or SET index.
